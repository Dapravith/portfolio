## Confirmed findings and proposed work

### P0 — Trust and accuracy

1. **Stale availability claim:** the homepage presents “Open to full-time and contract roles ... (as of 5 November 2025).” More than a year old, this reads as current. **Prepared fix:** for status older than 90 days, show a neutral “contact to confirm” message in both languages. Ask the owner to confirm actual availability before changing source data or marking it verified.
2. **Internship role grammar:** “Software Developer and DevOps Engineer Interned” and “Web Developer Interned” are awkward titles. **Prepared fix:** “Software Development & DevOps Intern” and “Web Developer Intern.” Verify the exact historical job titles against the résumé.
3. **Résumé freshness:** the `src/content/profile.ts` source cites the 5 November 2025 résumé. Confirm whether a 2026 résumé is available and update only with owner-reviewed source.
4. **Professional claims:** verify dates, employment names, ownership of work, project outcomes, and proof of personal contributions. Avoid implying restricted employer systems are public.
5. **Contact experience:** the current source uses a provider-enabled contact form with a mailto fallback. Verify production environment configuration by submitting a real test message with owner approval; do not assume live delivery.

### P1 — Senior-engineer storytelling

1. Strengthen the *first screen* with a crisp value proposition: actual responsibilities, architecture discipline, and demonstrated reliability.
2. Promote two or three strong case studies ahead of the full list; emphasize decisions, failure handling, trade-offs, and lessons learned.
3. Add meaningful screenshots/diagrams only when accurate and permitted. Avoid fake dashboards or architecture illustrations.
4. Link the most important technical claim to a source file, test, screenshot, or article when publicly available.
5. Reduce repeated explanations across “How I work” and the AI accountability table, especially on mobile.

### P2 — UI, responsive and technical quality

1. Review the live design visually in light/dark themes with viewport screenshots (320, 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440, 1920px), including real Khmer shaping and long labels.
2. Confirm that the mobile homepage does not feel excessively long; consider making the AI tool list scannable with fewer explanatory rows.
3. **Performance:** the repository's 9 November local Lighthouse notes report English homepage 95–97 Performance with LCP 2.38–2.83s; /km 91 Performance with LCP 3.20s. These are **repo-documented local tests, not independently verified field measurements**. Prioritize LCP and Khmer first-load behavior.
4. Maintain automated Playwright viewport and axe checks, but repeat on production and at least one real mobile device.
5. Validate external links, resume availability, metadata previews, email fallback, and correct homepage “last reviewed” provenance.

### P3 — Sustainable improvement

1. Keep the existing weekly GitHub dependency and link checks; avoid additional automation unless it catches an actionable failure.
2. Review portfolio content once a month; create a changelog of verified changes, not vanity updates.
3. Use privacy-conscious real-user field data before claiming production performance wins.
